# How to Write Rules and Skills

This document defines the purpose of rules in this repository and how to derive them. It applies when adding, modifying, or reviewing `AGENTS.md` and rule documents. The classification below is a repository convention; official guidance serves as supplementary reference material.

- Rules fall into two categories: **constraining errors** and **constraining practices**.
- **Reusability:** Rules must address recurring coding or work patterns and be likely to apply again. Do not turn one-time instructions or decisions specific to the current task into lasting rules.
- **Constraining errors:** When AI repeatedly exhibits the same error pattern, distill it into a concise constraint that primarily states what AI must not do. Guidance on what AI should do may be added when needed, but is not required. Example: `[no-silent-failure]` in [`coding-specification.md`](coding-specification.md) prohibits silent failures.
- **Constraining practices:** When AI repeatedly uses inconsistent or unsuitable approaches in the same specific scenario, derive a rule that prescribes a consistent implementation approach or scope of practice, with clear applicability boundaries. Practices or personal preferences explicitly chosen by the user can also justify constraints; they need not be presented as universal best practices. Example: `[e2e-scope]` in [`coding-specification.md`](coding-specification.md) limits E2E tests to real user workflows.
- **Content boundaries:** Retain only the constraints necessary to address these problems. Do not include general, routine, or dogmatic knowledge, or expand rules into coding tutorials. Rules may specify decision criteria, responsibility ownership, required ordering, and completion conditions to prevent the targeted errors. Detailed procedures for implementing those constraints, framework API usage, and technical references belong in skills. For example, `[port-copy-then-adapt]` in the [feature porting specification](feature-porting.md) requires copying before adapting and establishing behavioral equivalence before deleting, preventing omissions or unauthorized rewrites during porting.
- **Concise titles:** Where possible, give each rule a short title that directly identifies its subject. Avoid vague titles or titles that repeat the body.
- **Individual completeness:** Each rule should independently and coherently express one complete constraint, including the conditions and boundaries needed to interpret and apply it, without relying on other clauses to fill gaps. Separate rules may address different aspects of the same topic, but the essential parts of one constraint must not be scattered across multiple rules.
- **Scope and exceptions:** State the tasks, objects, or environments to which a constraint applies. Distinguish between prohibitions, defaults, preferences, and requirements that apply only under stated conditions. Define the conditions for legitimate exceptions rather than turning a local constraint into a blanket prohibition. For example, `[web-state-source]` in the [frontend specification](web-frontend.md) prohibits separately maintained copies of derived state but permits editing copies with explicit initialization, submission, and discard behavior.
- **Constrain behavior, not surface syntax:** Derive reusable decision criteria from concrete errors and cover semantically equivalent forms. Do not allow AI to evade a constraint by changing syntax, adding wrappers, or moving code. For example, `[try-catch]` defines boundaries based on error-handling responsibility and also applies to `Promise.catch()`; it must not be reduced to a ban on `catch`.
- **Layering and integration:** Keep one authoritative definition for shared constraints. Specialized rules specify only scenario-specific differences, and entry documents route readers by task. References help locate related rules; they do not replace essential content in the current clause. Use stable tags for rules that need to be referenced. Clearly identify the target to which project paths belong; they must not inadvertently point to the repository storing the specifications.
- **Rule consistency:** Rules must not impose contradictory requirements under the same conditions. Specialized exceptions must identify their applicability and the general requirements they override. When adding or modifying rules, inspect affected clauses and resolve conflicts rather than leaving contradictions for the executor to interpret.
- **Check unintended effects:** Check whether a rule prevents the targeted errors, standardizes the intended practice, and avoids obstructing legitimate behavior. Do not mechanically add splitting, documentation, testing, or approval requirements based solely on file count, line count, or phase count. Clauses requiring confirmation must distinguish unauthorized decisions from already-authorized execution steps to avoid repeated approvals or premature stopping.
